## Page 27

Paper Structure
Technology Format, the typical IEEE format:

- Title
- Abstract
- Introduction
- Methodology
- Results
- Discussion
- Conclusions
- References

## Page 28

An effective title should be specific, concise, descriptive, searchable, and relevant. Avoid acronyms in the title unless obvious.

## Page 29

Abstract:

- Concise summary of research conducted, results obtained, and conclusions reached.
- A stand-alone condensed version of the article.
- Typically 250 words or less.
- Uses keywords and index terms.

## Page 30

Keywords should be appropriate, applicable, specific, searchable, and logical. Use keywords in the title and abstract to improve discoverability.

## Page 31

Introduction establishes the research problem, motivation, prior work context, gap, and contribution.

## Page 32

Methodology explains problem formulation and the processes used to solve the problem, prove, or disprove the hypothesis. Use illustrations to clarify ideas and support conclusions.

## Page 33

Results summarize the data. Discussion interprets the results. Figures and tables should support the narrative.

## Page 34

Conclusion should explain what the research achieved relative to the problem stated in the introduction, summarize main findings, discuss implications, identify shortcomings, and suggest future research.

## Page 35

References should support and validate the hypothesis or problem addressed. Use only references that directly support the work. Ensure proper attribution and include complete bibliographic information.

## Page 36

IEEE encourages sharing research processes, methods, data, code, and findings to facilitate verification and reproducibility.

## Page 38

Peer review assesses quality, relevance, originality, validity, and significance before publication.

## Page 40

Editors and reviewers look for scope, validity, data correctness, clarity, compliance, advancement, and novelty.

## Page 42

Editors and reviewers focus on appropriate scope, clear original material, sound methodology, adequate figures and tables, and current relevant references.

## Page 43

Common rejection reasons include poor publication fit, scientific flaws, incorrect interpretation, poor writing or presentation, incremental content, insufficient field advancement, low quality, weak argumentation, or limited practical impact.

## Page 44

Reviewer-response strategy:

- Keep an open mind.
- Develop a point-by-point reply.
- Address each suggestion.
- Reference precise locations of changes.
- Respond in full sentences and paragraphs.
- Be respectful and tactful.

## Page 47

Avoid plagiarism. Cite and separate any verbatim copied material. Figure or table reuse may require permission. Credit ideas from other sources.

## Page 48

IEEE guidance states that use of AI-generated content in an article should be disclosed in acknowledgments, with the AI system identified and the sections and usage level described. Editing and grammar enhancement are generally outside the main intent but disclosure is recommended.

## Page 51

Duplicate submissions should be avoided. A conference paper can evolve into a journal article if the later article substantially extends the earlier work, cites it, and explains the differences.

## Page 52

Authors must submit original work that has not appeared elsewhere for publication, is not under review elsewhere, cites previous work, indicates differences from prior work, and informs the editor of related previously published work.
